01 — Read the room

How a committee moves.

From roll call to adjournment, here is the usual route. Your chair’s rules set the exact order.

01 / ROLL CALL

Be counted

The chair calls each country. Answer “present” or “present and voting”, as your rules require. The committee sets the agenda.

02 / FORMAL DEBATE

Open the GSL

A motion opens formal debate and the General Speakers’ List (GSL). Delegates give prepared speeches and state their country’s position.

03 / CAUCUSES

Focus & consult

A motion can suspend formal debate and move to a moderated caucus for focused speeches, or an unmoderated caucus to consult.

Make a decision

Field note 01 — The room

What is happening?

Keep these basics in your pocket. Your conference rules may use slightly different names or order.

01 / Debate

Ideas on the record

Delegates speak about the topic, explain their country’s position, and respond to proposals. The chair manages turns and procedure.

02 / Motion

A formal request

A delegate asks the committee to take a procedural action: change the debate format, move to voting, or end a session.

03 / Closure of debate

Stop debate on this item

A request to end debate on the current topic or draft and move toward voting. The chair may take speakers for and against first.

04 / Adjournment

End the meeting

A request to suspend or end the committee session. “Adjournment of debate” can mean a temporary pause; check your rules.

Easy distinctionClosure ends debate on an item. Adjournment ends or pauses the meeting. Neither one passes a resolution by itself.
Field note 02 — Motions

How to make a motion.

Wait until the chair asks for motions, raise your placard, and say the request in one clean sentence.

1Get the floor

Raise your placard when the chair opens the floor.

2Name the action

Say “Motion for…” and state the format or next step.

3Give the details

Include topic, total time, and speaker time when required.

4Yield to the chair

The chair rules, takes seconds or objections if needed, then announces the result.

Motion / pointWhen it helpsExample wording
Open formal debate / GSLStart formal speeches after roll call and agenda setting.“The delegate of [country] would like to raise a motion to open formal debate and establish the General Speakers’ List, with an individual speaking time of [time].”
Moderated caucusPause formal debate (the GSL) and hold short, timed speeches on one topic.“The delegate of [country] would like to raise a motion to suspend formal debate and move into informal debate, specifically a moderated caucus on the topic of [topic] for 10 minutes, with an individual speaking time of 30 seconds.”
Unmoderated caucusPause formal debate so delegates can talk and work together.“The delegate of [country] would like to raise a motion to suspend formal debate and move into an unmoderated caucus for 10 minutes to discuss [topic].”
Introduce a draft resolutionPresent a document once it meets the committee’s requirements.“The delegate of [country] would like to raise a motion to introduce Draft Resolution 1.1.”
Close debateEnd debate on the topic or draft and move towards voting.“The delegate of [country] would like to raise a motion to close debate on [topic / draft resolution] and move into voting procedure.”
Adjourn the meetingEnd the committee session, usually for the day.“The delegate of [country] would like to raise a motion to adjourn the meeting until [time / next session].”
Point of parliamentary inquiryAsk the chair how procedure works.“Point of parliamentary inquiry: could the chair clarify the speaking time?”
Point of personal privilegeRaise an immediate comfort or audibility issue.“Point of personal privilege: could the speaker please speak louder?”

Exact wording, voting thresholds, seconds, and precedence depend on your conference’s rules of procedure. If unsure, ask the chair before session.

Field note 03 — Drafting

Turn ideas into a resolution.

A draft resolution is a committee document proposing actions. Keep each clause clear, specific, and connected to the problem.

01
Agree on the problem

Gather evidence and find the shared concern your group wants to address.

02
Choose practical actions

Decide who does what, how it is funded, and how progress can be checked.

03
Write in clauses

Use short preambulatory clauses for context, then numbered operative clauses for actions.

04
Review and submit

Check format, sponsors and signatories, then follow the chair’s process to introduce it.

A tiny clause example

Operative clause

1. Encourages Member States to share early-warning data through existing regional coordination offices;

1. number clauses in orderEncourages begin with an action verb; end clauses with punctuation per your guide
Preambulatory clauses set context: “Recognising…”, “Concerned by…”, “Recalling…”
Keep it workablePrefer “who will do what” over “we urge everyone to solve the issue.” Avoid promises outside the body’s authority.

Field note 04 — Influence

Lead the work.
Earn the room.

As a delegate, you do not rule the committee—the chair does. You can shape where it goes by making useful contributions and helping others find common ground.

Know your brief.Carry your country’s priorities, limits, and one or two facts you can explain.
Speak to the gap.Listen for what has not been addressed, then make one focused proposal.
Ask, then adapt.Find out what other delegations need. Adjust wording without losing your core principle.
Make the next step easy.Offer draft text, connect people, and give credit. Follow through on what you promise.
Strong and diplomatic: state the concern clearly, support it with a reason, then offer a path forward.
03 — Tune your tone

Firm on the issue.
Open with people.

Diplomacy is clarity with room for others. Choose language that makes your position easier to engage with.

Listen first. Move with intent.
04 — Spot the procedure

Motions move the room.

A motion is a formal request for the committee to do something. Raise one when it helps the room make progress, and follow your conference’s rules on wording and precedence.

Moderated caucusFocused, timed speeches on a specific subtopic. State the topic, total time, and speaking time.
Unmoderated caucusInformal negotiation. State a useful purpose and a reasonable duration.
Point of informationA question to a speaker, if the chair permits it. Keep it brief and relevant.
Point of parliamentary inquiryAsk the chair a procedural question. Save policy arguments for debate.
Pocket reminder

Position. Proposal. Partnership.

Know what you believe, bring one workable next step, and leave the door open for another delegation to join.
